    How "scarred" is measured

    Burned land comes from NASA's MODIS burned-area product (MCD64A1). Greenness is MODIS NDVI, a satellite measure of how green the land is. A 1 km square counts as scarred when its August greenness is more than 0.05 below its own normal from before the fire, after subtracting how much never-burned land within about 25 km changed that year. That second step removes wet and dry years, so what remains is mostly the fire.

    When land burns again, it turns gray in its old fire year's bar and counts under the newer fire from then on, so no acre is counted twice. The percent above each bar is the share of that year's land, not counting land that burned again, that is back to normal greenness. About 4–5% of land that never burned also falls below the 0.05 line in a typical year, so a small part of the red is ordinary variation rather than fire damage.

    Wondering why we chose August? Click here

    Each year we compare one month's greenness to the land's normal, and August is the steadiest month. On the same land, greenness typically changes by about 0.02 from one August to the next, but by about 0.06 from one January to the next. That January swing is bigger than our 0.05 cutoff, so a dry winter would look like fire damage. Winter greenness mostly depends on when the rains came. By August the grass is brown everywhere, so the greenness left comes from trees and shrubs, which is where fire damage lasts.

    Land is first checked in the August after the year it burned. Most California fires burn between July and November, so the fire year's own August would catch only some of them, weeks after they burned. Waiting gives every fire one full winter and spring to grow back before its first check.
